Solar sensor build & wiring guide

Every connection for the solar variant — a Feather M0 that measures water temperature, harvests from a small panel through a current sensor, and runs on a protected li-ion pack. Read the three warnings first; one of them can destroy the board.

00

Read this first

These three bite hardest. The first is one wrong wire away from a dead board.

DANGER Check JST polarity before you plug the pack in. If you wired the pack yourself, verify + and − against the Feather's silkscreen. Reversed polarity on the battery connector can kill the board instantly. Never put panels in series either — two 5 V panels make ~12 V, over the charger's 7 V limit.
CAUTION The supercap is a momentary short until it charges. A ~1 F cap across a charged pack draws a large inrush the instant you connect it — enough to spark and stress a cell, or trip the protection board's over-current cutoff. Pre-charge it, or add a small series resistor during hookup. Details in section A.
CAUTION USB masks the panel — you can't watch solar over serial. Plugged in for programming, the USB pin sits at 5 V regardless of sun, so the INA219 reads USB, not the panel. To see real solar, run on battery and read telemetry over the air (FPort 11/21), or strap DEV with USB unplugged.

How it fits together

Two power sources meet at the Feather: the panel charges through the USB pin (via the INA219), and the protected pack runs and stores through the JST. The sensor is on A2; the INA219 talks over I²C.

Dashed = I²C (signal). Solid = power/sense. The full pad-by-pad list is in section 08.

A

Battery, protection & supercap

Do this first and carefully — it's the part with live energy. The pack is two cells in parallel (one 3.7 V group), the PCM protects it, and everything downstream hangs off the PCM's P+ / P−.

PCM 1SB+ B− · P+ P−
P+ / P−
Feather JST+ / −(GND)
  1. Parallel the cells. Tie both cell + terminals together, both − together. Charge both to the same voltage before joining them so they don't dump current into each other.
  2. Wire the pack to the PCM. Pack + → B+, pack − → B−. Follow the board's silkscreen exactly — reversed B±/P± can damage the protection chip.
  3. Attach the supercap across the pack (+ to pack +, − to pack −). To tame the inrush, touch it on through a ~100 Ω resistor for a second or two first, then connect solidly. It buffers the TX spike so a cold, high-resistance cell won't sag the rail below the 3.4 V brownout.
  4. Run the load off P+ / P−. Make a JST lead: P+ → JST +, P− → JST −. Do not plug it into the Feather yet — verify polarity with a meter first.
CHECKCutoff sits in the right window.2.54 V is below the Feather's 3.4 V brownout (so firmware always reacts first) and above the cell's damage point — exactly right. A charger is not a substitute: it stops over-charge, never over-discharge.
COLDLi-ion charges poorly below 0 °C.At this site the pack can freeze in winter. Charge current is tiny (~C/200), so it's gentle, just slow — a deep-winter refill may wait for the box to warm on a sunny day. Cells are treated as consumable on a 5–10 year cycle.
B

Solar charge path

The subtle part. The INA219 goes in-line in the panel's positive lead, so it measures the harvest current and the bus voltage the firmware keys on. The panel feeds the Feather's USB pin, which drives the onboard charger.

PANEL +5 V
anode ▸ bandSchottky
INA219Vin+ ▸ Vin−
Vin−
Feather USBcharger in
  1. Panel + into the Schottky. Panel + → diode anode; the banded end (cathode) points downstream, toward the INA219. The band blocks current flowing back into the panel at night.
  2. Through the INA219 shunt. Schottky cathode → Vin+; Vin− → the Feather's USB pin. Direction matters: current flows Vin+ ▸ Vin− when charging.
  3. Panel − to common ground. Panel − → Feather GND. All grounds are common.
DIRECTIONVin+ is the panel side, Vin− the Feather side.Backwards, getCurrent_mA() reads negative while charging — and the firmware floors negatives to zero, so your harvest would silently read 0 all day with no error to tell you why.
CHECKOne panel only; never in series.A "5 V" panel is ~6 V open-circuit — fine for the charger's 7 V limit. Two in series (~12 V) would exceed it. If you ever add a second panel, wire it in parallel with its own Schottky.
C

INA219 logic power & I²C

Separate from the sense terminals above, the INA219 needs logic power and two I²C lines. The firmware probes for it at address 0x40 — finding it is what selects the solar variant.

VCC3Vlogic supply (not the sense side)
GNDGNDcommon

WHYThe probe is what picks the variant.INA219 present → solar policy (FPort 11/21, 15-byte payload). Absent → the primary policy misreads the li-ion as a flat 6 V pack and parks at a 7-day interval. So a loose I²C wire looks like a working board that quietly stops reporting — the backend alarm watches for exactly that, and the boot fault frame (FPort 1/2) reports the probe's config register so a misdetect is visible from the first uplink. A soft-reset before the probe read guards the warm-reset case.
D

Water sensor

A single DS18B20 on the OneWire bus, with its mandatory pull-up. This is the reading that drives the whole seasonal interval.

Sensor pinout — DS18B20 (TO-92)

The bare sensor is a TO-92 package: one flat face and one curved back. Hold it with the flat face toward you and the legs pointing down — then the pins are GND, DQ, VDD, left to right. Swapping VDD and GND makes the part get hot and read nothing.

CHECKConfirm your probe's wire colours before trusting them.Most waterproof DS18B20 probes use red = VDD, yellow = DQ, black = GND — but some bring the data line out as white or blue, and cheap batches occasionally swap red and black. If in any doubt, continuity-buzz each wire to the TO-92 leg using the pin order above. Reversed VDD/GND overheats the sensor and reads nothing.
IDENTITYThe sensor announces its serial over the air.The boot fault frame reports the low 3 bytes of the DS18B20's ROM (ds18_rom). The production sensor is ffcf35 — record the new value whenever the sensor is replaced; a changed ROM on the wire means a swapped (or wrong) sensor.
DS18B20red·black·yellow

  1. Data to A2, with the pull-up. DATA (yellow) → A2, and a 4.7 kΩ resistor between DATA and 3V. Without it the open-drain bus never reads.
  2. Power the sensor. VDD (red) → 3V, GND (black) → GND.
  3. One sensor only on this bus. The firmware halts reporting to nulls if it sees more than one device on A2 — it can't tell which is the surface reading.
E

Radio jumper, mode strap & field reset

Three small but load-bearing connections: the LoRa stack needs DIO1 wired to a GPIO, one pin selects production vs development behaviour, and a reed switch gives the sealed box a magnet-operated hard reset.

  1. Jumper DIO1 → pin 6. Solder a short wire from the RFM95's DIO1 pad to Feather pin 6. MCCI LMIC requires it; the radio won't work without it.
  2. Set the mode strap on pin 11. Floating = PROD (deep sleep, USB detached, FPort 11). Tied to GND = DEV (no deep sleep, serial alive, FPort 21). Bench with DEV; deploy with PROD.
  3. Reed switch between EN and GND — normally-open (verify: OL with no magnet, continuity with). Mount it against the enclosure wall, test the magnet through the wall before sealing, and mark the spot outside. A magnet held for 3 seconds shuts the 3.3 V regulator down and power-cycles the whole rail — MCU, radio, sensor and INA219 — with no bootloader double-tap risk; release and it cold-boots, rejoins, and sends a fresh boot fault frame as confirmation.
NOTEPROD detaches USB — no serial in the field.That's deliberate for power. It also means the idle(750) defect only ever showed in PROD, never on a DEV bench — verify temperature behaviour on a PROD-strapped unit, not a DEV one.
WHY ENEN beats RST for the field reset.Grounding RST restarts only the MCU: I²C peripherals stay powered (the INA219's own reset threshold is 2 V), and a bouncing reed can double-tap the bootloader and strand the sketch. Grounding EN instead drops the whole 3.3 V domain — a true power cycle, every reset self-recoverable with another magnet pass.

09

Step-by-step connection guide

Every lead, in the order to run them. Do the low-energy wiring while the board is unpowered; build and verify the battery pack last, and don't plug it in until first power-up.

  1. Board prep (unpowered). Solder a short jumper from the RFM95 DIO1 pad to Feather pin 6 — the radio stack needs it, and it's easiest before anything else is attached.
  2. Board prep. Reed switch (normally-open — meter it: OL without magnet): one lead → EN, the other → GND. Support the glass body when bending the leads; fix it against the enclosure wall and mark the magnet spot outside.
  3. Sensor. DS18B20 DATA (yellow) → Feather A2.
  4. Sensor. A 4.7 kΩ resistor from that same DATA line → Feather 3V — the pull-up. The bus reads nothing without it.
  5. Sensor. DS18B20 VDD (red) → 3V.
  6. Sensor. DS18B20 GND (black) → GND.
  7. INA219 logic. VCC → 3V.
  8. INA219 logic. GND → GND.
  9. INA219 logic. SDA → Feather SDA (pin 20).
  10. INA219 logic. SCL → Feather SCL (pin 21).
  11. Charge path. Panel + (red) → Schottky anode (the plain, unbanded end).
  12. Charge path. Schottky cathode (the banded end) → INA219 Vin+.
  13. Charge path. INA219 Vin− → Feather USB pin. Direction matters — Vin+ is the panel side.
  14. Charge path. Panel − (black) → GND.
  15. Pack, off the board. Join both cells' + together and both − together — charge them to the same voltage first so they don't dump into each other.
  16. Pack. Pack + → PCM B+; pack − → PCM B−. Follow the board silkscreen exactly.
  17. Pack. Supercap + → pack +, − → pack − — touch it on through a ~100 Ω resistor for a second first, to tame the inrush.
  18. Pack. Make the JST lead: PCM P+ → JST + (red), PCM P− → JST − (black). Leave it unplugged.
  19. Mode. Strap pin 11 — floating for PROD, or tie to GND for a DEV bench.
  20. Verify. Meter the JST lead: correct polarity, ~3.7 V. Only then continue to first power-up.
  21. Verify the reed after first power-up: magnet on → everything goes dark; hold 3 s; release → normal boot and a rejoin. Test through the closed enclosure wall before final sealing.

Full schematic — every lead

One drawing with all conductors. Power taps meet the 3V rail, all returns meet the GND rail; a filled junction dot marks a real connection — wires that only cross don't touch.

positive / power (+) ground / negative (−) OneWire data SDA SCL strap reed ▷|  band = cathode

The Feather's JST − and GND pins are the same net internally — the battery return lands on JST −, every other ground on the GND rail.

CAUTION Vin− goes to +5 V — and that's correct. On the INA219, Vin+ and Vin− both sit on the positive rail with the shunt resistor between them; the "−" marks the load side of the shunt, not a polarity. So Vin− rests at ~5 V (the bus voltage the sensor reads) — which is why that lead is drawn red, not as a ground. High-side rule (per Adafruit): Vin+ → supply (panel), Vin− → load (the Feather's charge input). Current flows Vin+ → Vin−; reversed, it reads negative.
11

First power-up

  1. Meter the JST lead one more time — correct polarity, ~3.7 V — then plug the pack into the Feather.
  2. Flash the firmware (default build, INA219 present). On boot it probes 0x40, finds the INA219, and comes up as the solar variant.
  3. Confirm the variant from the uplink: FPort 11 (PROD) or 21 (DEV), 15-byte payload. If it appears on FPort 10/20, the INA219 wasn't detected — recheck the I²C wiring.
  4. Read the boot fault frame (FPort 1 PROD / 2 DEV, sent once after the first read): ds18_status: "ok", ds18_rom matching the attached sensor, faults empty, sane battery. It verifies the sensor, probe and pack wiring in one frame — from anywhere, no serial needed.
  5. See real solar only off USB: strap DEV, unplug USB, run on battery, and read the panel voltage and EWMA over the air. On the bench under USB the INA219 reads the 5 V USB rail, not the sun.
