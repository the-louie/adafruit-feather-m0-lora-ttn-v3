# The solar policy and v7 decoder (S04-*)

## What landed

`SolarPolicy` (li-ion + panel), the INA219 read path, and the v7 decoder for gisebo-05. gisebo-05 is the only target of all v3 work and it is solar, so this is the critical path, not a side variant.

## The three ideas that carry the design

**Key on bus voltage, not current.** The INA219 measures *harvested* current, which collapses to ~0 when the pack is full — in full summer sun, which with the claimed surplus is most of the summer. The charge-terminated test proves it end to end: 0 mA at panel Voc (6000 mV) reads as sun, keeps the EWMA up, and the decoder reports `panel_v ≈ 6.0` with a real clarity. If anyone ever "simplifies" the policy to key on current, that test fails.

**The window is time-based.** The EWMA decays against real elapsed seconds (the interval just slept), so its 24 h time constant is independent of the sampling rate. The convergence test is the direct proof: the same day/night cycle sampled at 5 min and at 6 h lands within 0.05. A wake-count window would hunt on a multi-day period, because the interval controls the window.

**Two gates, and the loop self-corrects.** The bonus needs `voltage_offset == 0` AND the latched sun bonus. So sun never shortens the interval on a struggling pack, and — the test that de-risks the unmeasured index-2 floor — as an over-aggressive pack drains past 3.85 V, the offset leaves 0, the bonus gate closes, and the interval returns to baseline *while the sun is still shining*. No explicit back-off logic; the physics does it.

## The gap the decoder closed

`interval_index` — byte 0, the sole output of the entire temperature/battery interval algorithm — was emitted by **no** prior decoder and mapped by **no** telegraf field. Three sprints of interval work computed a number the backend had never once seen. The v7 decoder emits `interval_index` and `interval_minutes`. This contradicted the design's own premise that uplinks are the only instrument, and it is now fixed.

## Day length is decoder-only

`clarity = ewma / expected_daylight_fraction`, computed in the decoder from the uplink timestamp and a per-device `SITE_LATITUDE_DEG`. Zero payload bytes; the firmware never computes day length and never stores latitude, which is exactly why it stays genuinely one-binary. A relocated unit needs a decoder-constant edit, not a reflash. Clarity is `null` when the clock is invalid — a ratio from an unseeded clock is nonsense.

## Not verified

No hardware. Everything is compile-verified plus host/decoder tests. Sprint 07 owns the whole solar bring-up: INA219 wiring and calibration, the bus-voltage-is-load-side assumption, the motorboat period vs the averaging window, and the li-ion battery-temperature coefficient against the 200 mV band spacing (S07-01). The self-correction and charge-terminated behaviour are argued and host-tested here; they become facts on the bench.
